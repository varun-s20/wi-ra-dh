01In plain language

What coordination is

Frequency coordination is the process of evaluating, assigning and managing repeater frequencies to minimize interference and make efficient use of the amateur radio spectrum.

Repeater frequencies are a limited resource shared by thousands of operators, clubs and organizations across Wisconsin and the United States. Without coordination, several systems could operate on the same or nearby frequencies in the same area, degrading performance for everyone. The goal is simple: allow as many repeater systems as possible to operate successfully while minimizing harmful interference.

  • A

    Why it is necessary

    Radio signals do not recognize county lines or state borders. A repeater on a tall tower may be heard dozens or even hundreds of miles away, so adding one takes more than picking an open frequency. Coordination considers:

    • Existing coordinated systems
    • Geographic location
    • Elevation and terrain
    • Antenna height
    • Transmitter power
    • Operating mode
    • Coverage area
    • Co-channel interference
    • Adjacent-channel interference
  • B

    What a coordinated repeater is

    A repeater whose operating parameters have been reviewed and approved through an established coordination process. Coordination generally includes assignment of a frequency pair, review of operating characteristics, evaluation of potential interference, documentation of system information and inclusion in coordination records.

  • C

    What coordination is not

    Coordination does not give ownership of a frequency. No operator or organization owns a repeater frequency. Coordination is a documented recommendation intended to promote efficient use of spectrum and reduce interference among systems in the same region — it is about cooperation, planning and responsible spectrum stewardship.

How coordination benefits repeater owners

  • Reduced interference risk and improved reliability
  • Documented coordination records
  • Technical review and guidance
  • Long-term spectrum planning that protects investment in equipment, sites and maintenance

How coordination benefits operators

  • More reliable repeater operation and better audio quality
  • Improved coverage and more communication opportunities
  • More efficient spectrum use
  • Stronger statewide repeater networks, including for ARES, RACES and SKYWARN

03Preparation

Before you begin

Guidance from WRA and the ARCS application guide.

  • You may request a specific frequency pair.

    Assignments depend on availability, engineering considerations and compatibility with existing coordinated systems. WRA may recommend an alternative that better supports spectrum efficiency and interference prevention.

  • You can leave the frequency blank.

    New coordination requests may leave frequency blank when ARCS is expected to recommend a suitable channel.

  • Choose the right request type.

    Select Modify Existing Coordination only when changing an already coordinated station.

  • Use a valid FCC callsign.

    ARCS verifies the license and auto-populates available FCC information when you enter a valid callsign.

  • Save a draft if you need to.

    If you still need to gather technical or contact information, save a draft in ARCS and return later.

  • Certification comes at submission.

    Certification is required only when the application is submitted or resubmitted.

  • Submit significant changes before you make them.

    Site relocation, antenna height changes, power increases and similar modifications should be reviewed before implementation.

  • Analog and digital are both coordinated.

    WRA coordinates analog and digital repeater systems, subject to available spectrum resources and technical compatibility.

08Questions

Coordination questions

All questions

Who makes coordination decisions?

Coordination reviews are performed by individuals authorized by the Wisconsin Repeater Alliance and operating under policies established by the Board of Directors. Decisions are based on established technical standards, engineering analysis and documented policy rather than individual preference.

How long does coordination take?

Coordination times vary with the complexity of the request and the availability of spectrum. Simple applications may be processed quickly, while requests involving heavily occupied bands, large coverage areas or potential interference may require additional engineering review. WRA aims to provide timely, thorough evaluations for every applicant.

Is WRA affiliated with the FCC?

No. WRA is an independent nonprofit amateur radio organization and is not part of the Federal Communications Commission. However, amateur repeater coordination is recognized by the FCC under Part 97 rules, and coordinated systems may receive certain protections not afforded to uncoordinated systems.

Can clubs own coordinated repeaters?

Yes. Many of Wisconsin's repeater systems are owned and operated by amateur radio clubs, technical organizations, emergency communications groups and other nonprofits. WRA actively supports club-owned systems.

Will my personal information be made public?

No. While certain system information may appear in public repeater directories, personal contact information and administrative records are handled in accordance with Alliance policies and applicable privacy requirements.

Have a question before you apply?

We can help you understand the process and what you will need. Include your callsign and site location if your question is about a specific repeater.
