Wisconsin Repeater Alliance, Inc.

Amateur radio frequency coordination for Wisconsin

WRA provides fair, transparent and engineering-based frequency coordination for repeater owners, radio clubs and amateur operators across the state. The engineering work runs on ARCS, the coordination platform WRA developed and operates.

02Coordination process

How coordination works

Every application follows the same five stages. ARCS performs the engineering analysis; WRA reviews it under established policy and makes the decision.

  1. 01

    Step 1: Prepare

    Applicant

    Gather station, site, antenna and contact details using the pre-application checklist.

  2. 02

    Step 2: Submit

    Applicant · in ARCS

    Submit the application online in ARCS. Drafts can be saved until you are ready.

  3. 03

    Step 3: Engineering Review

    ARCS

    ARCS runs the engineering analysis — conflict checks, interference and coverage studies.

  4. 04

    Step 4: WRA Review / Decision

    WRA

    Coordinators authorized by WRA review the application under Board-established policy and decide.

  5. 05

    Step 5: Record

    WRA · in ARCS

    The coordination is recorded, and system details appear in the public repeater directory.

03Organization and platform

WRA is the coordinating body. ARCS is how the work gets done.

Two names, one coordination service — never interchangeable. Decisions stay with WRA and its governing structure.

The organization

Wisconsin Repeater Alliance

WRA is responsible for amateur radio repeater frequency coordination in Wisconsin, and for the policy, governance and oversight behind it.

06Public service

Supporting public service and emergency communications

Wisconsin's repeater infrastructure plays a critical role during severe weather, public service events and emergency response. Coordinated systems provide dependable communications for these networks.
