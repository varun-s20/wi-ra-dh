01

Stage 01

Prepare

Collect the station, site, antenna and contact details the application asks for.

What happens
You gather technical and contact information using the pre-application checklist. You may identify a preferred frequency pair, or leave it for ARCS to recommend.
Who is responsible
Applicant
What you provide
  • Transmitter callsign, holder, trustee and sponsor details
  • Site location, latitude and longitude
  • Band, output and input frequency, tone and operating mode
  • Antenna height, pattern, power, gain and losses
What happens next
Sign in to ARCS and start a New Coordination request.
Timing
At your own pace — ARCS lets you save a draft and return later.
02

Stage 02

Submit

Submit the application online in ARCS.

What happens
You complete the ARCS application and certify it. Entering a valid FCC callsign lets ARCS verify the license and auto-populate available FCC information.
Who is responsible
Applicant in ARCS
What you provide
The completed application and certification. Certification is required only when the application is submitted or resubmitted.
What happens next
The application enters the review process. It may require additional technical information.
Timing
Online, whenever you are ready.
03

Stage 03

Engineering Review

ARCS runs the engineering analysis the review is based on.

What happens
  • Automated frequency, distance and coverage conflict checks
  • Longley-Rice interference analysis
  • Automatic effective radiated power (ERP) calculation
  • FCC license lookups
Who is responsible
ARCS WRA coordinators
What you provide
Additional technical information, if the review requests it.
What happens next
The results go to WRA for review and decision.
Timing
Varies with complexity. Stage timeline from WRA coordination policy
Simple applications may be processed quickly. Heavily occupied bands, large coverage areas or potential interference require additional engineering review.
04

Stage 04

WRA Review / Decision

Individuals authorized by WRA review the application and make the decision.

What happens
Reviews follow policies established by the Board of Directors. Decisions rest on technical standards, engineering analysis and documented policy — not individual preference. WRA may recommend an alternative frequency that better supports spectrum efficiency and interference prevention.
Who is responsible
WRA
What you provide
Responses to any questions or revision requests. Applications awaiting applicant revision are tracked in ARCS.
What happens next
A decision on the application. Approved coordinations are recorded.
Timing
Varies with complexity. Stage timeline from WRA coordination policy
05

Stage 05

Record

The coordination becomes part of Wisconsin's coordination records.

What happens
The coordination is documented in ARCS and included in coordination records. System information may appear in the public repeater directory; personal contact information and administrative records are handled under WRA policy.
Who is responsible
WRA records in ARCS
What you provide
Keep your record current: contact information, equipment status, operational status and technical parameters.
What happens next
Periodic record reviews. Submit significant modifications before implementing them.
Timing
Ongoing, for the life of the system.
M

Existing station

Modification

Significant modifications should be submitted for review before implementation.

Changes that need review
  • Site relocation
  • Antenna height changes
  • Power increases
  • Frequency changes
  • Coverage expansion
  • Changes in operating mode
  • Addition of linked systems
How to submit
In ARCS, choose Modify Existing Coordination. The request follows the same engineering review and WRA decision stages as a new coordination.
Start a modification in ARCS (opens in a new tab)
R

Existing station

Renewal and record reviews

WRA may periodically review coordination records to keep information current and accurate.

What you may be asked to verify
  • Contact information
  • Equipment status
  • Operational status
  • Technical parameters
In ARCS
ARCS supports self-service renewals and record ownership management.
Off the air?
If a coordinated repeater will be off the air for an extended period, notify WRA so records stay up to date and spectrum is managed responsibly.
Sign in to ARCS (opens in a new tab) Notify WRA
I

Existing station

Interference complaint

If another repeater is causing interference, WRA can help. Many concerns are resolved through communication, engineering review and voluntary corrective action.

  1. Talk with the other owner

    WRA encourages cooperation among repeater owners. A direct conversation often resolves the issue.

  2. Report it to WRA

    Send an interference report with your callsign, your site location and details of the system causing interference.

  3. WRA reviews

    WRA reviews coordination records and technical information related to the report and seeks a practical, technically sound solution.

Report interference

Not sure which path applies?

Contact WRA with your callsign and site location and we can point you to the right process.
