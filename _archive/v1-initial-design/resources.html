About WRA

What is the Wisconsin Repeater Alliance?

The Wisconsin Repeater Alliance, Inc. (WRA) is a Wisconsin nonprofit corporation dedicated to the coordination and stewardship of amateur radio repeater spectrum throughout the state. It is governed by a nine-member Board of Directors made up of Wisconsin amateur radio operators and community volunteers committed to fair, technically sound and transparent spectrum management. As a federally recognized 501(c)(3) nonprofit, WRA operates exclusively in support of the amateur radio community and the public benefit.

What is ARCS, and how does it relate to WRA?

ARCS, the Amateur Radio Coordination System, is the technology system developed and operated by WRA to perform the engineering and coordination functions — propagation modeling, interference studies, database management, application processing and coordination workflows. WRA is the organization; ARCS is its platform. Coordination decisions remain with WRA. About ARCS.

Is WRA affiliated with the FCC?

No. WRA is an independent nonprofit amateur radio organization and is not part of the Federal Communications Commission. However, amateur repeater coordination is recognized by the FCC under Part 97 rules, and coordinated systems may receive certain protections not afforded to uncoordinated systems.

Why is WRA organized as a nonprofit?

WRA was established as a Wisconsin nonprofit corporation and operates as a 501(c)(3) charitable organization so that repeater coordination remains focused on service to the amateur radio community rather than private interests. This structure provides public accountability, organizational continuity, transparent governance, long-term stability, and the protection and preservation of coordination records.

How is WRA funded?

WRA relies on membership dues, coordination-related support, donations, sponsorships and other lawful sources of support. All revenue supports the mission of coordination, spectrum management, record keeping, education and community service.

How can I support the Wisconsin Repeater Alliance?

Repeater owners, clubs, businesses and individual operators can support WRA through membership, volunteer service, technical expertise, donations, sponsorships and participation in committees and programs. Become a member or volunteer with us.

Coordination

Why is frequency coordination important?

Repeater frequencies are a limited shared resource. Through technical review and engineering analysis, coordination promotes reliable repeater operation, reduced interference, efficient spectrum use, fair access to available frequencies and long-term protection of repeater investments.

Who makes coordination decisions?

Coordination reviews are performed by individuals authorized by WRA and operating under policies established by the Board of Directors. Decisions are based on established technical standards, engineering analysis and documented policy rather than individual preference.

How do I apply for a new repeater coordination?

Apply online in ARCS. You will be asked for your callsign, site location, antenna information, proposed frequency pair, operating mode, power levels, coverage objectives and contact information. Once submitted, the application enters the review process and may require additional technical information. See Information you will need.

Can I request a specific frequency pair?

Yes. Assignments depend on availability, engineering considerations and compatibility with existing coordinated systems. In some cases WRA may recommend alternative frequencies that better support spectrum efficiency and interference prevention.

How long does the coordination process take?

Coordination times vary with the complexity of the request and the availability of spectrum. Simple applications may be processed quickly, while requests involving heavily occupied bands, large coverage areas or potential interference may require additional engineering review. The coordination process.

What if I need to change my repeater?

Significant modifications should be submitted for review before implementation — including site relocation, antenna height changes, power increases, frequency changes, coverage expansion, changes in operating mode, or the addition of linked systems. Modification path.

Does coordination need to be renewed?

WRA may periodically review coordination records to keep information current and accurate. Repeater owners may be asked to verify contact information, equipment status, operational status and technical parameters. Renewal path.

What happens if my repeater goes off the air?

If a coordinated repeater will be inactive for an extended period, owners are encouraged to notify WRA. Accurate records support responsible spectrum management and keep the coordination database up to date.

Does WRA coordinate digital repeaters?

Yes. WRA coordinates both analog and digital repeater systems, subject to available spectrum resources and technical compatibility.

What if another repeater is causing interference?

WRA can assist by reviewing coordination records and technical information related to reported interference. WRA encourages cooperation among repeater owners and seeks practical, technically sound solutions. Many concerns can be resolved through communication, engineering review and voluntary corrective action. Interference path.

Can clubs own coordinated repeaters?

Yes. Many of Wisconsin's repeater systems are owned and operated by amateur radio clubs, technical organizations, emergency communications groups and other nonprofits. WRA actively supports club-owned systems and recognizes their important role in serving local communities.

Will my personal information be made public?

No. While certain system information may appear in public repeater directories, personal contact information and administrative records are handled in accordance with Alliance policies and applicable privacy requirements.

03Volunteer

Help shape the future of amateur radio in Wisconsin

WRA is a volunteer-driven organization. Whether you are a long-time repeater owner, an RF engineer, a club officer or newly licensed, there is a role for you.

Why volunteer

  • Preserve and protect Wisconsin's repeater spectrum
  • Improve coordination services for repeater owners
  • Support emergency communications infrastructure
  • Promote good engineering and operating practices
  • Assist fellow amateur radio operators
  • Strengthen the future of amateur radio in Wisconsin

Board and leadership

Members in good standing may serve on committees, take part in leadership programs and run for Board positions as vacancies occur.

Professional skills are welcome

Not every role requires technical expertise. WRA welcomes experience in accounting and finance, nonprofit administration, legal and regulatory matters, information technology, graphic design, writing and communications, fundraising and sponsorship development, and event planning.

Complete the form and a member of the leadership team will help identify opportunities that match your interests and skills.
